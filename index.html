Norman Borlaug

The agricultural scientist whose work helped save millions of people from hunger around the world.

“The destiny of world civilization depends upon providing a decent standard of living for all mankind.” — Norman Borlaug

The story

More than a list of achievements.

1914

Norman Ernest Borlaug was born in Cresco, Iowa, in the United States.

1942

He earned his PhD in plant pathology and began research that would later transform global agriculture.

1944

Borlaug moved to Mexico to work with farmers and develop disease-resistant wheat varieties.

1960s

His high-yield wheat helped increase food production in countries including India and Pakistan during the Green Revolution.

1970

He received the Nobel Peace Prize for his efforts to reduce hunger and improve food security around the world.

2009

Borlaug passed away at age 95, leaving a legacy of science, persistence, and service to humanity.

His legacy

Science can be an act of service.

Norman Borlaug’s work reminds us that knowledge becomes more meaningful when it is used to improve other people’s lives.
